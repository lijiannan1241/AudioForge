Podcast Post-Production: 40 Minutes Down to 4

Every episode repeats the same denoise, EQ and loudness pass. That is not craft, it is a pipeline. Write it as a preset and spend the saved time on the edit.

2026-11-17 · 603 words

中文版 → 中文

Where the forty minutes go

A 60-minute episode typically burns: 5 minutes on denoise (plus parameter guessing), 3 on de-essing, 5 on EQ, 3 on compression, 5 on loudness normalization, 10 on export and checks — with "does this sound louder than last week?" sprinkled in between.

The slow part is not any single step. It is having to decide every parameter again, by ear, every time. Yesterday -18 dB denoise, today -12 dB, and now the show sounds like a different show each week.

A fixed order matters more than the settings

  1. Denoise (spectral, ~12 dB) — remove room noise first, or the compressor lifts it along with the voice;
  2. De-ess (dynamic 5–8 kHz, ~10 dB) — sibilance gets harsher after compression, so it goes before it;
  3. Compression (threshold -18 dB, ratio 3:1) — tame dynamics for commuting listeners;
  4. Three-band EQ — cut 100–200 Hz for mud, lift 3–5 kHz for intelligibility, never more than +2 dB;
  5. Loudness normalization (-16 LUFS) + true-peak limiting (-1 dBTP) — the delivery spec, last;
  6. Format conversion + chapter markers — MP3 or M4A, with intro/topic/outro marked.

Step 5 must be last for a simple reason: normalize first and then add +2 dB of EQ, and your final loudness is 2 dB off. Everything upstream was wasted.

Save it as a preset, then just drop files

In LoudPipe those six steps are one JSON preset (podcast_master). Each episode becomes three actions: drop the recording, pick the preset and format, press start. Then read the report.

What actually saves the time is the production plumbing:

  • Concurrency — -j 4 when an episode arrives as several segments;
  • Retries — --retries 2, so a flaky network share does not restart the whole run;
  • Failure isolation — one bad segment, the rest still ship; retry only the failure;
  • Naming templates — output lands as EP042_seg01.mp3, no manual renaming;
  • Chapter markers — --marker intro=0 --marker topic=72.5 writes MP3 CHAP or QuickTime chapters, so clients can skip;
  • Reports — input/output LUFS, true peak and timing per file, as CSV / JSON / HTML, archived per episode.

The same run from the CLI:

loudpipe run -p podcast_master -i ./EP042 -o ./out --format mp3 \
    --denoise 12 --deess 10 -j 4 --retries 2 \
    --marker intro=0 --marker topic=72.5

"One click" does not mean "no listening"

A preset guarantees consistency, not magic. Still check three spots: the first 30 seconds (noise floor), a loud laugh or shout mid-episode (over-compression), and the ending (truncation).

The payoff is invisible: nobody says "great post this week", but ten episodes in a row that never tire the ear is the preset working. A show whose loudness drifts loses listeners around episode three, and you never learn why.

Two problems it also solves

Quiet guests. Per-file normalization means no manual gain per person.

Noisy remote recordings. Learn a profile from a room-tone sample (loudpipe noise-profile) and apply it to the whole episode — steadier than fixed settings.

Forty minutes to four is not a lower standard. It is deciding once instead of deciding every week. The thirty-six minutes you get back are enough to rewrite the cold open three times.
